# Write, cleanup, destination and provider-session recovery — 0.150.0

This batch extends the independent C# engine. Full replacement acceptance remains open, and production startup still uses the original engine.

Publication retains the complete validated plan during interrupted sibling-file writes and atomic receipt replacements. Existing outputs are not moved until every sibling has been flushed and checked. A restarted incomplete installation restores its checked preimages and validates source data again before processing. A committed receipt reuses the complete output set. Forced workers stop halfway through writes, after flushing, and after replacement; ordinary I/O and cancellation failures are also tested. An interrupted rollback can restore its remaining preimages on another retry.

Archive temporary filenames are registered in the durable publication receipt before writing begins. Partial archive writes leave the prior archive intact; a committed archive remains idempotent, and tracked abandoned temporary files are removed during completion. Unrelated temporary files are not searched or deleted.

Cleanup first validates the outputs and backups, then removes publication siblings and workspace entries while retaining the committed receipt until last. A crash between any tested deletion and removal of the receipt can replay cleanup. The empty-workspace and already-removed-workspace gaps are recognized through the recovery locator. Recovery never follows filesystem links. Receipt readers permit atomic replacement without preventing a concurrent writer from replacing its file.

A bounded, checksummed locator in the request's configured output directory records the owned workspace, scope, processing recipe and source identity. It supports rendered nested, absolute and parent-relative destinations without needing another provider or media request after archive completion. Locators are flushed and atomically replaced, their interrupted writes are cleaned on retry, and completed locators are removed. Matching archived jobs can reconcile cleanup even if the caller changes the output template or processing options. Pending uncommitted jobs are retained. Edited locators, outputs, backups and invalid paths fail instead of deleting recovery data.

For pre-locator receipts, bounded discovery descends through ordinary folders in the configured output directory and useful literal output-template prefixes. It does not scan an entire volume, follow links, or infer arbitrary old absolute locations. Limits are 2,048 locators/workspaces, 8,192 visited folders and 16 nested levels. Legacy rollback folders without a verifiable publication plan remain refused.

Wistia, JW, Brightcove, Kaltura and Vimeo initial reads, renewals and queued reads are guarded by job-owned cancellation and draining gates. Disposal prevents late results or waiters from establishing a new lease. Repeated reads retain the registered canonical resource set; renewal updates the lease's effective URLs, so a second read cannot silently bypass renewal with an unregistered signed URL. PeerTube account/file sessions gain the same lifetime protection, rejected-generation single-flight renewal, failed-grant preservation and fixed verified resource/format scopes. Kaltura validates a replacement grant's account binding and expiry before installing it; a rejected or canceled grant cannot become the cached session. Disposed HTTP owners cannot recreate provider session state.
