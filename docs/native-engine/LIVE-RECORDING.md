## Implemented

The native recorder captures new complete segments while the playlist advances, before the sliding window can discard them. It deduplicates overlapping sequence identities, refuses missing or changed segments, preserves AES keys/ranges/initialization and uses existing static timeline assembly for captured discontinuity groups. Captured resources are replayed locally; ffmpeg receives local files and performs fixed-demuxer full decoding before the recording can replace an output.

Normal recording starts at the last three currently available segments. `--live-from-start` requires a complete beginning exposed by an EVENT playlist with initial sequence zero; it does not invent expired earlier segments. `--no-live-from-start` restores near-edge recording. The native extension `--live-duration SECONDS` bounds media time to complete segment boundaries, up to 24 hours; an ended event may finish sooner. Without a duration, recording continues until ENDLIST, cancellation, a stalled playlist or the 24-hour session bound. Cancelling does not publish an incomplete recording or record archive success.

Target duration controls polling and stall detection. Zero/missing live targets and segment durations beyond their declared target or recording limits are refused. Completed short ffmpeg playlists retain their previous compatibility, including a zero target duration that is not used to drive an open recording. Delta playlists remain unsupported rather than being misread as complete windows. Recording resources and keys are cleaned on completion/failure/cancellation, while prior published files and archives are protected by the existing transactional pipeline.

Saved/job argument order, rate limiting, fragment retries, buffers, chunk/range delivery, selected audio/video modes, source retention, callbacks and known-identity archive skips remain integrated.
